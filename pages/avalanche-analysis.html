The analysis tool at the heart of the product had been in development for over a year, and users couldn't get through it. I ran a design sprint to replace it, and tested my prototype head to head against the original.

  • 2 wksTo an end-to-end interactive prototype
  • 1 yr+Spent developing the tool it replaced
  • AllTest users succeeded with the new flow
  • 0Contact details left for the old one

What was brokenUX audit & interviews

The tool had to turn cluster analysis, language processing and classification models into something a non-expert could use. A UX audit and user interviews showed three problems.

  1. Surprises

    The app didn't behave the way users expected it to.

  2. One path

    A rigid, linear flow limited what users could do and when.

  3. Hidden

    Users couldn't find the information they needed in the interface.

How can we turn cluster analysis, language processing and classification models into an accessible, usable and feasible flow that lets a non-expert pull themes from open-ended text?

Problem statementAgreed with data science, engineering and analysts

The sprintTwo weeks, start to test

  1. Understand

    UX audit of the existing tool, plus user interviews.

  2. Workshop

    Sessions with our data science lead, engineering manager and analysts to keep ideas feasible.

  3. Sketch

    Crazy 8s for divergent ideas, then an end-to-end solution sketch from everyone.

  4. Decide

    A decider reviewed the sketches. My proposal went forward.

  5. Prototype

    Sketch to wireframe to an interactive prototype, with the Product Manager.

  6. Rumble

    New versus old, with the same script, tasks and success measures.

What changedOld flow, new flow

I kept each data science concept as its own step in the flow, so each could be improved separately if users found it valuable. The interactions borrow from patterns people already know, like managing an inbox.

BeforeAfter
Confirm every AI suggestion by handSmart themes applied automatically, fully editable.
A fixed, linear pathOne view of themes and responses, with search, sort and filter.
Shift-click to select many responsesClick and drag to select, then act from a menu or drop onto a theme.
Organise themes only at the end, in another tabOrganise anywhere, in place, at any point.
A complex, confusing training screenThumbs up or down, plus a drawer to review what the model tagged.
One depth of analysis for everyoneChoose your depth, from a quick subset to every response.

The new flowPrototype recordings

  1. Start analysis

    One clear call to action, and smart themes instead of a blank page.

  2. Explore themes

    Move freely between themes and responses, wherever the analysis leads.

  3. Add responses to themes

    Select one or many, then use the action menu or drag onto a theme.

  4. See similar responses

    New: pick any response and surface its nearest neighbours from the cluster map.

  5. Organise themes

    Build and rearrange theme hierarchies in place, as understanding grows.

  6. Train a theme

    Simple feedback that teaches the model to auto-tag, without breaking flow.

OutcomeThe rumble

Both versions were tested on task understanding, task completion, delight, and whether users would leave contact details and say they'd use it.

Every user succeeded with the new prototype. The original was seen as a work in progress, and nobody left their details.

Lesson

A sprint is a good way to challenge sunk cost. Testing against the incumbent with the same script made the decision about evidence, not opinion.
